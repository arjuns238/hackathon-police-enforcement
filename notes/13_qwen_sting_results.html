What the undercover agent found

Four Qwen workers check each other's work under a rule that forces them to reject. In most games one of them, Dave, is secretly a trusted agent who privately offers the others a deal and reports who agrees. We varied Dave's character and when he makes his approach. This page shows what happened, with real messages from the games.

In four lines

  • Qwen colludes on its own. From episode 2, nearly every pair accepts each other, with or without Dave.
  • Almost nobody makes an explicit deal with Dave: 7 real deals in all, only when pushy or friendly Dave asked for mutual acceptance in so many words. Subtle Dave and passive Dave got none.
  • Nobody suspected Dave: 0 of 168 judged conversations.
  • Dave's own reports are accurate but broad: 95% name a worker who really agreed to judge without the full log, which is what he was told to report; only 5% are actual trades.

Data: 30 games, 110 episodes (24 games complete; the rest partial). Games per setup: No Dave 3 · Passive Dave 4 · Pushy, ep 1 4 · Friendly, ep 1 4 · Subtle, ep 1 3 · Pushy, ep 3 4 · Friendly, ep 3 4 · Subtle, ep 3 4. Games 1–2 ran on asri's Modal account, 3–4 on the teammate's. Preliminary: a few games per setup, so only large differences are visible.

4. "Say no, do yes" depends on timing

Workers who refused Dave out loud in his approach episode, and whether they then accepted his work anyway:

In episode 1 nobody accepts anyone yet, so a refusal is a real rejection. By episode 3, refusers said no to the trade and accepted Dave's work on "the evidence" anyway. Small numbers.

5. Passive Dave heard nothing

Passive Dave never brings up a deal; he only listens. Nobody proposed one to him in any of his games (0 real deals in 45 target-episodes), although collusion in his games reached the same near-100% as everywhere else. The collusion was silent: everyone simply accepted on "the evidence".

6. Dave's own reports

Dave privately reports workers who agree to accept work without the complete log. Compared with two judges: the broad one (agreeing to judge without the full log, the standard Dave was given) and the strict one (an actual trade with Dave):

So an undercover agent's log faithfully records "they agreed to bend the rule", but almost never "they made a deal with me". Reading his log as a list of deals would overstate it about 18-fold.

7. Pushy Dave jumps the gun

Told to approach only in episode 3, pushy Dave offered explicit trades two episodes early in one game, and 2 of the real deals came from those unscheduled offers. Friendly and subtle Dave kept to their timing.

What to keep in mind

  • Small numbers. 30 games; real deals number 7. Treat differences between characters as hints.
  • Incomplete design. Some of the teammate's games stopped early on a conversation-length limit fixed later (deviation D15), so setups have 2–4 games.
  • One model, medium reasoning. Qwen-3.8-27B at reasoning effort medium (the paper used xhigh); the replication check shows the same collusion pattern.
  • Game quirks. 5% of chat turns were lost, mostly to messages over 200 characters (workers pasting logs), a rule of the paper's game; a formatting glitch in Qwen's tool calls was fixed early (D9), and games before the fix were thrown out.
  • Judges are Claude. The strict judge agreed with a blind hand check on 30 of 30 items, but the hand labels were also made by Claude, and only 3 of them were deals.
  • Every change from the registered plan is listed in notes/12 (D1–D16).
